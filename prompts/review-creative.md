# Creative Writing Review

Review creative work written by an AI agent: stories, dialogue, character voice, persona prompts, copy, names, or interpretations of a work. Look for choices left unresolved, interchangeable details, formulaic phrasing, and explanations that repeat what the work already conveys. Judge these against the brief and genre, not a fixed preference for plain or understated writing. Produce a report identifying what to delete, what to return for rewriting, and what the brief was missing.

Brief: {The user's original request. If absent, state “No brief provided” in the first line and limit category A to contradictions within the draft.}
Reference material: {Source text, samples, or earlier drafts the user approved. If absent, state “No reference material provided; voice matching not assessed” in the first line and skip checks that require those references.}

## Principles

1. **Judge the work itself.** The author's explanation of their choices and self-assessment are not evidence. If something is not in the draft, claiming it in an explanation does not put it there.
2. **Use the name-swap test where specificity matters.** In passages meant to distinguish a character, place, or product, replace its name. If the passage remains interchangeable, identify the missing details. Do not require every sentence to establish identity.
3. **Judge style against the brief and references.** Compare sentence length, wording, tone, and genre conventions with the requested result, not with your own preferred style. A requested change from the samples is not a mismatch.
4. **Report patterns and their effects.** Repetition, metaphor, symmetry, and aphorisms are not defects by themselves. Report a pattern when it erases distinctions the work needs, repeats an effect without developing it, or conflicts with the brief. Give counts and locations.
5. **Do not rewrite the work yourself.** Mark text for deletion or return it for rewriting and state what is missing. Do not supply replacement sentences; this review is a report, not a new draft.
6. **Check the brief before blaming it.** If a missing description or source contributed to a problem, identify it. If the brief already covered the point, report the failure to follow it. Recommend the description, material, or explicit constraint needed to resolve the finding, not a general ban on a stylistic device.
7. Do not flag the author's choices themselves. Subject, stance, warmth or distance, length, and narrative style are not problems unless they conflict with the brief or fail to achieve the effect claimed in the work.

## What to look for

These are leads, not a checklist to fill out item by item.

### A. Failure to choose
- Several versions or directions offered for the user to choose from when one finished draft was requested
- Incompatible approaches combined without resolving their conflict, leaving the requested direction unclear
- Interpretation or criticism that lists observations but withholds a judgment the evidence supports
- A clear direction in the brief watered down in the draft

### B. Interchangeable details
- Trait labels doing all the characterization where the task calls for a distinctive person
- Stock scenes or actions replacing details needed to establish this setting or situation
- Generic emotion words where the requested effect depends on a specific reaction
- Names and copy that fail to distinguish the product as the brief requires

### C. Formulaic rhetoric
- The same polished ending repeated across paragraphs without adding a new effect
- “Not A, but B” used to reject a claim neither the work nor its context has established
- Parallel phrasing or symmetrical sentences repeated mechanically rather than serving the requested rhythm
- Metaphors that obscure the intended meaning or repeat an image without developing it
- Characters whose speech becomes indistinguishable through shared rhetorical habits, when the work requires distinct voices

### D. Explaining the work
- A narrator or character spelling out the theme without the brief asking for that treatment
- Emotional summaries that repeat a character's shown reaction rather than adding the interior perspective the form calls for
- An ending that adds a summary lesson or moral without the brief asking for it
- Unrequested explanations of creative choices or highlights appended to the draft

### E. Material outside the form
- Formatting that conflicts with the requested form or its conventions
- Assistant acknowledgments and closing offers surrounding the work: “Sure, here is…” or “Would you like me to adjust the tone?”
- Disclaimers and qualifications that have no role in the brief or intended use
- Chat messages rendered as formal prose when the task calls for conversational messages

### F. Voice (requires reference material)
- Sentence length, wording, or tone that differs from the requested reference voice without a corresponding instruction; identify the reference passages
- Verbal tics, interjections, or punctuation exaggerated beyond the references when faithful voice matching is requested
- A trait turned into a formula: sarcasm or a catchphrase that appears occasionally in the references used in every sentence
- Wording attributed to a source as an exact quotation that cannot be found there; original dialogue is not a source quotation merely because it uses quotation marks

### G. Persona and character prompts (only when reviewing these)
- Prohibitions that substitute for a usable description of the character's voice, motives, and behavior
- Invented examples presented as source dialogue, or examples that contradict the requested characterization
- Abstract principles that fail to distinguish the intended character
- Response tables that flatten distinct situations into canned replies instead of supporting the required behavior
- Operational instructions that contradict the requested persona or switch it off without the brief calling for that change
- Missing setting or relationship information needed for the requested interaction
- Clauses that unintentionally replace the character's voice with a generic assistant voice

For an established character, test whether someone familiar with them could recognize the characterization without the name. For an original persona, test whether the prompt supplies the distinguishing traits and behavior the brief asks for.

### H. Working process (only when an execution record is provided)
- Responding to a request for a revised draft with self-critique instead of the revision
- Reintroducing rejected choices during revision despite explicit feedback
- Claiming “the requirements are now met” when the cited problem remains unchanged

## Report format

First line: scope, whether you checked against the brief and references, and the conclusion (“N passages to rewrite, M to delete” or “Ready to use”).

Then list findings in order of impact, grouping instances of the same pattern:

- **Text:** Quote it or give locations such as “the endings of paragraphs 2, 4, and 5.” Include counts for recurring patterns.
- **Category:** A–H.
- **Evidence:** Which required distinction is missing, which reference passage it differs from, which part of the brief it contradicts, or what effect the repeated pattern undermines.
- **Action:** Delete / return for rewriting (state what is missing: which kind of detail or source material) / user decision required.

Then list **Missing from the brief**, if any: each gap that contributed to these problems, written as either a description ready to add to the brief or a source the user needs to provide. Do not list requirements already stated in the brief as missing.

Write nothing beyond these three parts. If the draft is ready to use, write only the first line. Do not append generic suggestions.
